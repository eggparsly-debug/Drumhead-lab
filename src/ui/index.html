What's being simulated

Heads are circular membranes with bending stiffness, solved as Bessel-function modes. Each has a frequency, mass, and damping that come from the material, thickness, tension, and air loading. Uneven tuning splits the paired modes, so you hear beating.

The air inside is a shared spring that couples both heads' "breathing" modes. A port, open bottom, or goblet stem adds a moving slug of air (a Helmholtz resonance), so a djembe gets its bass and a kick gets its port.

The striker is a mass with a nonlinear (Hertzian) contact. Hit harder and the contact gets shorter and brighter. Big hits also raise the head tension, which bends the pitch.

Snare wires are small masses bouncing against the head, and every impact makes buzz. Shell and hoop are ring and cylinder modes driven by the head's pull on the rim. Shell material also sets edge losses and how much the cavity absorbs.

Cymbals, hats & gongs are curved bronze (or any alloy) plates with hundreds of bending modes. Hard hits push energy up through the spectrum in an energy-conserving cascade, so they bloom into wash. A cubic stiffening term bends the pitch. Hi-hats are two plates on a clutch spring that clash at their edges, and rivets, chains, holes, lathing and hammering each change the modes and damping.

Every hit acts on the same vibrating drum, so rolls and flams interact, just as they would in the VST.
